MNP-KB v0 · surface charge of micro- and nanoplastics · commit

What zeta measurements can and cannot tell you

MNP-KB v0 is a table of surface-charge measurements of plastic particles (the zeta potential, in mV, and a few raw electrophoretic mobilities), read out of open-access papers. Next to every number it stores the five conditions that number depends on, and when a paper did not state one, it stores that absence as a fact instead of guessing a default. Think of a sensor reading logged without its calibration or ambient conditions: the value exists, but you cannot compare it with anyone else's. Each section below is one picture of one claim.

    0 · How it works

    A model reads the papers; code decides everything else

    MNP means micro- and nanoplastics. The project turns open-access papers into one table where every surface-charge value carries its conditions, the address of the line it came from, and an explicit “not reported” wherever the paper is silent. Only one step uses an AI model to judge. Every other step is plain code that anyone can rerun and get the same bytes.

    If you have worked with energy-market data: a price means nothing without its delivery period, its unit and its time zone, and you cannot recover the time zone from the price itself. Ionic strength is the time zone of a zeta potential. It is also the condition papers leave out most often.

    The original idea: read the curve, not the dot

    TriedUnify published zeta values: shift each paper’s single dot to one common condition.Blocked. Shifting a dot needs its ionic strength, the condition papers leave out most (section 6). Even the complete case 14 still disagrees.
    BuiltThe curve method: soft- and hard-particle physics (Ohshima), and inference by MCMC and a neural posterior estimator trained on simulated curves.Works on simulations. The physics matches an independent recomputation, and simulation-based calibration passes (README, D4 notes).
    Not doneRun it on measured salt series; report mobility at one reference condition, and the plateau.No measured curve has gone through it. Papers print dots; full salt series exist mostly for reference particles such as sulfate latex, silica and bacteria.

    How to read this

    A hard particle carries its charge on its skin: salt hides it, and mobility falls toward zero. A soft particle carries charge inside a layer water flows through: salt cannot hide it, and mobility stops at a plateau set by the layer’s charge density and how hard water flows through it. One measurement at one salt level sits where the two curves can meet.

    What it means

    Zeta was never the goal. Measure across salt, fit the physics, and report two numbers: mobility at one reference condition, comparable across labs, like a solar panel quoted at standard test conditions; and the plateau, which stays defined under a protein corona, where zeta has no shear plane.

    The pipeline, step by step

    codedeterministic, no model, same output every run modela Claude agent reads and judges
    1. Fetchcode

      What happensOne search query to Europe PMC: hits, of them open access, full texts downloaded once and packed with a checksum manifest.

      What it guaranteesThe corpus cannot drift. Europe PMC is not an archive, so the texts are never fetched again (CP-01, CP-02).

    2. Convertcode

      What happensEach paper’s XML becomes plain text with numbered lines. Every paragraph gets an address made from a hash of its own content.

      What it guaranteesA value can point to the exact line it came from, for almost no extra tokens. Raw XML would cost several times more (CP-03, CP-04).

    3. Scancode

      What happensRegular expressions find every printed number that could be a zeta potential or a mobility. The scanner knows nothing about plastics.

      What it guaranteesThe reader starts from a list of candidates, so a value is not lost because a model skimmed past it (EX-01).

    4. Readmodel

      What happensClaude worker agents read each paper under a written brief, stored by its hash. For every value they decide which particle, which liquid and which conditions it belongs to, and return the value, a verbatim quote and the line addresses. A condition the paper does not give is written down as “not reported”, never guessed.

      What went wrongAgents fill the structure they are given: a value cited from another study became a measurement, a storage temperature became the measurement temperature, an n appeared that the paper never states. Each was caught by a check against the data, not by trusting the agent (EX-09, EX-10).

    5. Verifycode

      What happensThe quote must appear word for word on the cited lines and the number must sit inside it; units are read from the article. A row that fails is dropped and counted. Three rules Martin ratified then clear defaults that slipped through: a printed zero is not an ionic strength (R1), a storage or step temperature is not the measurement temperature (R2, R2b), a buffer’s concentration is not its ionic strength (R3).

      What went wrongA check that flags a correct value teaches the agent to delete it. Guards that missed molar units, kelvin or powers of ten pushed correct values toward “not reported”. They were fixed test-first, and a worker now never deletes a printed value to clear a flag (EX-12, EX-13, EX-14).

    6. Decidecode

      What happensEvery pair of rows is checked: the same measured quantity, the same particle description, and every condition stated and matching within a tolerance. The printed value itself is never used as evidence. Henry’s formula turns a missing input into an interval, never into a default.

      What it guaranteesComparability is computed, not judged, so anyone can rerun every verdict and the census of section 6 (CM-01, CM-03, HM-01).

    7. Evaluatemodel

      What happensThe model becomes the test subject. One reader, , answers the same questions twice: once handed the table (cell D), once handed keyword search over the papers’ lines (cell B).

      What it showsWhere retrieval over text fails: comparability lives in the join of two rows, and no single passage contains it (section 8, GR-15).

    8. Rebuildcode

      What happensreport reproduce rebuilds every committed table and document offline, with the network refused, and compares the result byte for byte.

      What it guaranteesThis page is built from one commit; no number on it is typed by hand, and of them are cross-checked against the review guide (PR-02).

    Three ideas to keep

    Absence is a value

    “pH not reported” is a fact about the paper. A blank cell would hide it, and a default would lie about it. Each row carries one true-or-false flag per condition.

    Compare pairs, not numbers

    One zeta value is neither right nor wrong on its own. Two rows are comparable only when their conditions match, and that is a property of the pair, which no single passage of text holds.

    A model reads; code decides

    An agent’s output is a claim with an address. Code checks it, applies the rules and makes every decision, so no result rests on trusting an agent.

    Words used on this page

    Zeta potential (ζ)
    The electric potential, in mV, at the particle’s slipping plane in a liquid: how charged the particle looks to its surroundings. It belongs to the particle in that liquid, not to the particle alone.
    Mobility
    What the instrument actually measures: how fast the particle drifts in an electric field. Zeta is computed from it.
    Ionic strength
    How much dissolved salt the liquid holds, in mM. It sets the Debye length.
    Debye length (κ⁻¹)
    The thickness of the cloud of ions around the particle. More salt, thinner cloud.
    Henry’s factor f(κa)
    The factor that turns mobility into zeta. It runs between the Hückel and the Smoluchowski limits and depends on particle size against the Debye length, so it needs the ionic strength (section 5).
    Fully conditioned
    A row that states all five conditions: medium, ionic strength, pH, temperature, instrument.
    Comparable pair
    Two rows whose difference can be blamed on the particle, because everything else is stated and matches.
    Lost only to absence
    A pair of rows on the same particle where nothing either row states differs, but a condition is missing, so no one can decide.

    2 · The five conditions

    Almost no measurement comes with all five conditions it depends on

    A zeta value is read against five conditions: the medium (the liquid), its ionic strength (dissolved salt in mM; it sets how far the particle's charge reaches into the liquid), its pH, the temperature, and the instrument. Missing one is like a voltage reading with no load stated.

    4 · Papers, not rows

    Reporting is a habit of the paper, so the honest sample size is the number of papers

    Each paper's share of rows that state a condition

    How to read this

    A bubble is a group of papers with the same number of rows (x, log scale) and the same share of those rows stating the selected condition (y). Bubble area is the number of papers in the group. Pick a condition above the chart.

    What it means

    Nearly every bubble sits on the 0 % or the 100 % line: a paper states a condition for all its measurements or for none. That is what an ICC of means (ICC, intraclass correlation: the share of the variation that lies between papers; 1 would mean every paper is all-or-nothing). So rows are worth only about independent rows.

    5 · The correction formula (Henry)

    Most zeta values come with an unstated conversion that makes them uncertain by up to a factor of 1.5

    The instrument measures mobility: how fast the particle drifts in an electric field. Zeta is computed from it with a factor f that runs from 1.0 (the Hückel limit: a small particle in a thick cloud of ions) to 1.5 (the Smoluchowski limit: a large particle in a thin cloud). The right f depends on κa, the particle radius measured in Debye lengths (the thickness of that ion cloud), so it needs the ionic strength and the size. If a paper does not say which factor it used, the true value lies anywhere between ζ ÷ 1.5 and ζ × 1.5.

    Every zeta row with a printed value: reported number and where the true value can lie

    reported value corrected value: an interval corrected value: one number nothing to plot: rows ( print no number, mobilities lack a temperature)

    How to read this

    Each thin vertical line is one of zeta rows, sorted left to right by its reported value (the grey curve). The orange line spans the values the truth can take; a blue dot marks a row whose corrected value is a single number. Hover to read a row.

    What it means

    The orange fan is the norm: of rows name no conversion model and lack κa, so a reported −30 mV means anything from −20 to −45 mV. Only zeta rows can be pinned to one number.

    How many rows land in each case

    one number an interval nothing

    How to read this

    Z cases are zeta rows, M cases are mobility rows (converted to zeta here), N rows print no number at all. The model counts as named when the paper's full text mentions it, which is a mention, not proof it was applied.

    What it means

    Totals: rows give one number, an interval, nothing. That is why the released dataset carries no harmonised column: an interval is not a value.

    What this does not prove
    
      
    Read every number above with these limits
    
    
    
    
      
      
    • That the values are physically true. A row is a reported measurement. Its quote was checked against the paper; the paper was not checked against reality.
      • 
      
    • That it represents the literature. One database, open access only:  of  Europe PMC hits. The shares are shares of that subset.
      • 
      
    • That the extraction is accurate. The gold set it is scored against ( rows,  papers) was annotated by a model; rows re-judged by a human: .
      • 
      
    • Which conversion model a paper applied. The scan finds mentions. Every corrected single number assumes the mention is the model used.
      • 
      
    • The Debye length of media with divalent ions. κ⁻¹ assumes a 1:1 salt in water at 25 °C, so κa is underestimated for CaCl₂ or phosphate.
      • 
      
    • How a reader performs in general. One reader model, one run per cell, on this project's own questions. The agentic cells, where the reader chooses what to fetch, have not run.
      • 
      
    • That the comparability verdicts are the field's. The rules and tolerances are this project's own; no one outside it has ratified them.
      • 
      
    • That two comparable rows hold the same particle. The check compares the printed description; supplier, charge density and ageing that were not printed are invisible to it.
      • 
      
    • How environmental micro- and nanoplastics behave. The particles measured are synthetic ( of rows), and  rows come from air or dust.
