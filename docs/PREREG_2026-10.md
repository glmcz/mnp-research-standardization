## Two things that are not the same

| | what it is | where it comes from | may it use the test data? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **prediction** | what we expect before looking | physics, published values, other datasets | **no**, or the test proves nothing |
| **ground truth** | what is actually correct, used to score | the papers themselves, human-curated data, Martin's labels | it *is* the data |

So a dataset cannot give us the prediction for its own test. It can give the prediction for a
**different** test, as a prior from another matrix or study. And some datasets can serve as
ground truth: see the end of this file.

## P1 · H3a on soil: a finer lower size limit, a higher reported concentration

- **Data:** the global soil dataset (Wu et al., Zenodo 20806151, 297 studies). Not yet analysed by
  this project.
- **Statistic:**
  - one point per study: the study's finest lower size limit, and its median positive
    concentration;
  - Spearman ρ between log lower limit and log median;
  - the OLS slope of log10(median) on log10(lower limit), with a study-bootstrap interval.
- **Prediction:** ρ < 0, with ρ between −0.15 and −0.40, and a slope between −0.3 and −1.2.
- **Where it comes from:**
  - **ρ:** the same statistic on two other matrices, NCEI ocean water −0.26 and MP-SED sediment
    −0.25 (`tool/p2/data.json`).
  - **Slope:** physics. If particle counts follow a power law n(d) ∝ d^−α, the count above a lower
    limit d scales as d^(1−α). The slope is therefore −(α − 1), and α between 1.3 and 2.2 (the
    literature range D3 uses: Kooi & Koelmans 2019, Koelmans et al. 2020) gives −0.3 to −1.2.
- **Refuted if:** ρ ≥ 0, or the slope interval excludes the whole −0.3 to −1.2 band.

## P2 · H3a on soil: rescaling does not settle co-located disagreement

- **Data:** the soil dataset's 0.1° grid cells that hold two or more studies (201 cells according
  to the recon).
- **Statistic:** per cell, the spread (max − min) of log10 concentration across studies, before
  and after rescaling each study to 1–5000 µm at α = 1.6 with D3's `powerlaw` code. Report the
  median relative reduction.
- **Prediction:** the median reduction is under 25 %.
- **Where it comes from:** D3, drinking water and freshwater, 34 studies. Rescaling at α = 1.6
  narrows the measured spread from 8.26 to 7.53 decades, which is 9 %
  (`tool/viz/data.json`, `d3`).
- **Refuted if:** the median reduction is 50 % or more.

## P3 · H3c: one slope does not fit all measured size distributions

- **Data:** MPsizeBase v19-5-2026 (Zenodo 20832480), 203 measured particle size distributions.
- **Statistic:** a power-law α per distribution, fitted on its bins by least squares in log space,
  using distributions with 4 or more bins.
- **Prediction:**
  - the median α falls between 1.3 and 2.2;
  - 30 % or more of the distributions fall outside 1.6 ± 0.3;
  - atmosphere and ocean medians differ.
- **Where it comes from:** the literature range D3 uses (above). The spread is a prediction of
  method diversity.
- **Refuted if:** 80 % or more of the distributions sit within 1.6 ± 0.3.
- **A worked example, already seen, so it is not a test.**
  - *Corrected 2026-10-19, after the commit, from the datasets' own PANGAEA abstracts.* The first
    version called the Weser pair "a 10 µm filter vs a 300 µm net" measuring the same thing. It
    is in fact two complementary size fractions of one study: S-MP 11–500 µm (a pump with a
    500 µm pre-filter onto a 15 µm screen, then µFTIR imaging) and L-MP 500–5000 µm (a 300 µm
    net, then ATR-FTIR).
  - With those windows, the power law predicts a small-to-large ratio of 11.9 at α 1.6, 103 at
    α 2.2 and 3 049 at α 3.1. The observed median over the 22 stations with both fractions above
    zero is 5 237, which implies α ≈ 3.24 for this river. *(5 340 corrected to 5 237 on 2026-10-19: one station with no large particles had added an infinite ratio to the median.)*
  - Rescaling at α = 1.6 would therefore undercount its small particles by roughly 440-fold.
  - This supports P3's direction but was seen first, so it is exploratory. P1–P6 are
    unchanged.

## P4 · H2b: descriptors predict little of a literature zeta value

- **Data:** the 1 046 rows of `out/tables/p2_zeta_size.csv`. These rows were already seen
  descriptively (H2a), so this test is confirmatory in **method only**, not in data. That is
  reported as such.
- **Statistic:** gradient-boosted trees; 5-fold cross-validation grouped by paper; out-of-paper R²
  per feature set: {size}, {polymer}, {surface}, {liquid class, pH}, {all}.
- **Prediction:**
  - R²(all) < 0.5;
  - R²({size}) ≤ 0.05;
  - R²({surface}) and R²({liquid, pH}) each exceed R²({polymer}).
- **Where it comes from:** Yang et al. 2026 report a zeta model with test R² 0.69 on 31 particles
  under controlled conditions (their SI, Table S2). Literature values in unstandardised liquids,
  with ionic strength missing for over 90 % of papers, should score lower.
- **Refuted if:** R²(all) ≥ 0.69, or {polymer} or {size} out-predict both {surface} and
  {liquid, pH}.

## P5 · Judges: tier 1 is one family, not two

- **Data:** Nimble (local) and Microsoft-Decision-1 (OpenRouter), on Martin's 30 labels plus the
  traps.
- **Statistic:** the phi correlation of their error indicators; the share of tier 1's confident
  errors that the tier 2 jury repeats.
- **Prediction:**
  - phi ≥ 0.5, because both are post-trained from Qwen3.5-9B;
  - the jury repeats 50 % or more of tier 1's confident errors.
- **Where it comes from:** the shared base model (vendor pages, verified 2026-10-19), and the
  follow-up summary of arXiv 2609.29769 (not yet verified).
- **Refuted if:** phi < 0.2.

## P6 · H5: estimation, not a directional test

No published value predicts how often a single model reader gives a confident wrong cause on
this task. H5 therefore **estimates** the false-explanation rate per configuration (V0–V3), with
intervals, and makes no directional claim. The only commitment made now: the V0 → V2 difference
is reported whatever its sign.

## Ground truth: what scores the results

| question | ground truth | independent of our pipeline? |
|---|---|---|
| a value or condition a paper prints | the paper's own text (code-checked: verbatim quote, value in quote) | yes |
| our extracted zeta values and liquids, overall | **ToMEx 2.0**, curated by people (the paper describes 60+ volunteers), for the papers both datasets hold | **yes**, a second, human route. The overlap size will be counted before use |
| why a value contradicts its label (Q2) | **Martin's 30 labels**, done blind | yes; no dataset records causes |
| whether two records are the same measurement (Q3) | the published tables (Reisser Table S1, Eriksen figshare) | yes |
| a judge's verdict | Martin's labels plus the trap set | yes |

ToMEx is the closest thing to the dataset Martin asked about. It cannot supply predictions, but it
can independently check our extraction on the papers we share. That also answers part of OD-03.

### What the failed parts say

- **P4, liquid + pH.** Across papers, the liquid class and pH predict zeta worse than the mean
  does. A class such as "salt solution" spans 0.1 to 500 mM of ionic strength (slide 8), so the
  class alone carries too little of what matters. That fits H1. It does not rescue the
  prediction, which failed as written.
- **P3, the median.** The pre-registered fit used every bin. See the secondary analysis below.

### Secondary analyses, chosen after seeing the results (descriptive, not confirmatory)

- **P2, the zero.** It comes from the 51 cells whose studies all used the same window, where
  rescaling changes nothing. In the 112 cells where the windows differ, the median change is
  −4.5 %, and rescaling **widens** the disagreement in 54.5 % of cells.
- **P3, the detection limit.** MPsizeBase's own "Low LOD size filter" drops the bins near the
  detection limit, where counts fall off. Fitted on the filtered bins (148 distributions):
  - median α 1.67, and still only 22 % within 1.6 ± 0.3;
  - atmosphere 1.67 vs ocean 1.73, p 0.0072.
  - Our α ranks the distributions as MPsizeBase's own does (ρ −0.82, where the sign is their
    convention); the scale differs, and the definition is not resolved.
- **P1, implied slope.** A cross-study slope of −0.655 implies α = 1 + 0.655 ≈ 1.66, the same
  value the filtered MPsizeBase median gives. On average 1.6 is right, but for any one study it
  is wrong about three times in four.
- **P4, leakage.** The same model with rows shuffled across papers scores R² 0.302 instead of
  −0.074. Rows from one paper teach the model their own answers. Paper identity alone (per-paper
  means) scores 0.069, so it is near-duplicate rows, not lab averages.
