# FINDINGS — query kind E: may these two measurements be compared?

**2026-09-18.** Code: `src/mnp/eval/comparability.py`. Tests: `tests/eval/test_comparability.py`
(54). Query set: `queries/kind_e_comparability.csv` (320 pairs), regenerated by
`uv run python -m mnp.eval.comparability`. **Zero model tokens were spent building any of it.**

---

## The headline

**Of the 1 785 105 unordered pairs of rows in `d1_zeta_table.csv`, 16 are fully comparable.
That is 0.00090 %. None of the 16 crosses a paper boundary. Ten of the 16 compare
electrophoretic mobilities; only 6 compare zeta potentials, and those 6 come from 3 papers.**

The corpus holds 1 834 reported zeta potentials from 491 papers. Every one of the
1 680 861 possible zeta-against-zeta comparisons is blocked except six, and all six are
within-paper. **No zeta potential in this corpus can be compared with a zeta potential from a
different paper.** That is not a shortage of comparable pairs; it is zero, and it is the
abstract's thesis stated as a count.

`conditions_complete = 62/1890 = 3.3 %` already said the context is usually missing. This says
what that costs: the context is missing *per row*, and comparison needs it in **two** rows at
once, so the 3.3 % squares before any other rule is applied.

The next tier down is not empty. **35 pairs are `conditionally_comparable`** — everything is
stated and the rows differ only in a named, quantified condition, so they sit on a common axis
(a titration, not a comparison). 23 of the 35 are within a paper and **12 cross papers**. So
cross-paper work in this corpus is possible, but only as "here is the same particle at a
different ionic strength", never as "here are two measurements of the same thing".

---

## 1. What "comparable" is defined to mean

> `comparable(a, b)` is true when a difference between the two reported numbers is
> attributable to the **particle** — not to the measurand, not to the context, and not to the
> unstated model that converted one of them.

Three verdicts, and the third is derived from the blocker set, never computed separately:

| verdict | meaning |
|---|---|
| `comparable` | no blockers |
| `conditionally_comparable` | every blocker is a *stated, quantified* difference in medium, ionic strength, pH or temperature. Same particle, same instrument, same measurand, same conversion regime — a curve, not a comparison |
| `not_comparable` | anything else |

A blocker carries a `kind`, and the distinction is the dataset's whole point:

- **`unreported`** — the paper never said. No reader can repair it and nothing may default it.
- **`undecidable`** — the paper said something that is not a number two rows can be compared on
  ("from pH 2 to 7", "room temperature").
- **`mismatch`** — both rows state it and they differ.

---

## 2. The rules, and why each one

### R1 · Same measurand — `quantity_mismatch`

A zeta potential and an electrophoretic mobility are not the same quantity. Mobility is
measured; zeta is a model-dependent conversion of it, and Smoluchowski and Hückel differ by a
factor of **1.5**. The extraction brief already forbids converting between them
("Zeta potential and electrophoretic mobility are different quantities. Never convert"). The
rule is the pair-level restatement of that. Blocks **5.75 %** of pairs.

### R2 · Same instrument — `instrument_unreported`, `instrument_mismatch`

The schema's own justification for the field: *"Different instruments apply different
mobility-to-zeta conversions."* E2b in `docs/FINDINGS.md` measured that **5.6 %** of papers
name the conversion model at all, so for 94.4 % of the corpus the instrument is the **only**
available proxy for it. Two rows from different instruments have had two unknown conversions
applied, and the difference between their numbers is not attributable to the particle.

A family-level string ("Zetasizer", no model) keys to the family and therefore does *not* match
a named model. The brief records family level as a distinct, weaker statement; this rule keeps
it weaker rather than quietly promoting it.

### R3 · Particle identity is not the polymer name — `material_*`, `functionalisation_*`

The abstract: MNPs are not a homogeneous class; behaviour comes from polymer, chemistry,
functionalisation and surface state *together*. PS-COOH, PS-NH₂ and bare PS latex are different
particles at the same nominal size. So `material` alone never establishes identity, and
`functionalisation` participates as a first-class identity field, not as a note.

**A missing `functionalisation` is `unreported`, never `BARE`.** 41.7 % of rows do not state
one. A paper that does not say whether its particles carry carboxyl groups has not said what it
measured, and `functionalisation_unreported` blocks **66.1 %** of all pairs on its own.

### R4 · A condition either row fails to report blocks on that condition

The five conditions of `MeasurementConditions`: medium, ionic strength, pH, temperature,
instrument. Each already carries a per-field justification in the schema, and each is checked
in **both** rows. Nothing defaults, ever. In particular:

- "DI water" / "Milli-Q" is a **reported medium** and an **unreported ionic strength**. The
  test `test_di_water_is_a_reported_medium_and_an_unreported_ionic_strength` exists to keep it
  that way; 567 rows (30 %) are `DI_water`, so the temptation is not hypothetical.
- A pH range is not a pH (`pH_not_numeric`). For a functionalised particle the sign flips
  across the isoelectric point, so "from pH 2 to 7" transfers nothing.
- "Room temperature" is not a measurement temperature (`temperature_not_numeric`). The brief
  already rules that an incubation, culture or storage temperature is not one either, so
  `temperature_C` here is a measurement temperature by construction.

### R5 · The conditions must also *agree* — the tolerances

Reporting a condition makes a row interpretable; **matching** it is what makes a difference
attributable to the particle. Each tolerance is a claim, so each is stated:

| rule | tolerance | why that number |
|---|---|---|
| `ionic_strength_mismatch` | 10 % relative | κ⁻¹ ∝ I^(−1/2), so 10 % in I is 5 % in the Debye length — inside replicate scatter |
| `pH_mismatch` | 0.2 units | reported pH carries one decimal; 0.2 admits rounding, not a different buffer point |
| `temperature_mismatch` | 2 °C | η of water moves ≈2 %/°C and ζ tracks η/ε, so 2 °C is ≈4 % in the conversion |
| `conversion_regime_mismatch` | 5 % in Henry's *f* | *f* spans 1.0→1.5, the factor the abstract names; 5 % is one tenth of that gap |

These four are the `_AXIS_CODES`: a pair blocked by nothing but these is
`conditionally_comparable`, because the difference has a name and a number.

### R6 · Medium — the decision, and what it costs either way

**`medium_class` governs the verdict; `medium_text` is then checked *within* a matching class.**
Two blockers, never both: `medium_class_mismatch` when the classes differ, else
`medium_text_mismatch` when the normalised texts differ.

- **Class alone is too coarse.** `culture_medium` collapses "CV growth medium" and "HP growth
  medium" — two compositionally different media from the same paper (PMC11726606), differing in
  ionic strength by 5×. Class alone would call them the same medium and invent a comparison.
- **Text alone is too brittle.** It is free text; "PBS" and "phosphate-buffered saline" are the
  same medium spelled twice.

So the class is the gate and the text is the tie-break, and the cost is stated rather than
hidden: `medium_text_mismatch` is the **sole** blocker on 21 pairs. Dropping it entirely raises
the comparable count from 16 to 37. That is the price of the strict half of this decision, and
it is 21 pairs out of 1.78 million.

**What is lost either way:** the class cannot see a composition, and the text cannot see that
two compositions are equal. Neither reads the medium as chemistry. A medium whose composition
*is* printed already gets its ionic strength recorded as derived (the brief's C1 ruling), and
that is the only part of "medium as more than a string" this key actually uses.

### R7 · The conversion regime — `size_measured_unreported`, `conversion_regime_mismatch`

Applied to **zeta rows only**: mobility is what the instrument measures, so no conversion sits
between two mobilities.

ζ is obtained from μ through Henry's function *f*(κa), which runs from 1.0 (Hückel) to 1.5
(Smoluchowski). Since 94.4 % of papers do not name which limit they used, the reader cannot
check the model — but the *regime* is computable from what is reported:

```
  kappa^-1 = 0.304 / sqrt(I[M])  nm        (1:1 electrolyte, water, 25 degC)
  kappa*a  = (size_measured / 2) / kappa^-1
  f(ka)    = 1 + 1 / (2 * [1 + 2.5 / (ka * (1 + 2*exp(-ka)))]^3)      (Ohshima)
```

Two rows whose *f* differ by more than 5 % have had materially different physics applied,
whether or not either paper says so. The radius is the **measured** hydrodynamic size and never
the nominal one — nominal is the supplier's claim, and `ZetaRow` does not carry
`size_nominal_nm` at all, so it cannot be substituted by accident.

**This rule is the one that is derived rather than read**, and it is the one a reviewer should
argue with first. Its cost is small and reported: dropping the whole family raises 16 → 29.

It also produces the sharpest single illustration in the corpus. PMC11099890 reports both μ and
ζ for the same five particle sizes under identical conditions. All ten μ-pairs are comparable.
Of the ten ζ-pairs, **two** are — the other eight cross a conversion regime (κa runs 4.1 → 35.5
across the series, *f* 1.12 → 1.41). The paper's own size series is internally comparable in the
quantity it measured and not in the quantity it published.

### R8 · The value is never evidence — structural, not merely tested

`ZetaRow` has no `value`, `value_text`, `sd`, `n` or `unit` field. A rule that reads the number
**cannot be written**, because the number is not in the record. Chemically distinct particles
converge to similar ζ after natural organic matter or corona adsorption while remaining
different in surface chemistry and biological behaviour — the abstract's central point — so a
similar value is not weak evidence of a similar particle, it is no evidence.

Three tests pin it (`test_the_row_carries_no_reported_value_at_all`,
`test_value_columns_do_not_reach_the_row`, `test_similar_values_never_repair_a_blocked_pair`),
and the mutation in §5 shows all three bite.

### The canonicalisers, and their one direction of failure

`material_key`, `functionalisation_key` and `instrument_key` merge **spellings only**. None
drops a modifier to reach a match; a string not in the explicit table keys to itself
(`literal:…`) and therefore matches nothing but an identical string. An unrecognised spelling
costs a comparable pair; an over-eager merge invents one. Only the first failure is acceptable
in a key.

| field | distinct strings | distinct keys | canonical | literal | not reported |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| material | 151 | 100 | 90.2 % | 8.5 % | 1.3 % |
| functionalisation | 375 | 296 | 29.8 % | 28.4 % | 41.7 % |
| instrument | 361 | 83 | 76.1 % | 12.8 % | 11.2 % |

The functionalisation column is where this bites: "carboxylate; POPC lipid corona",
"UV-aged; Cu2+ adsorbed onto surface prior to zeta measurement", "gold core; 14 days UV-aged".
Those are not surface *groups*, they are surface *histories*, and no vocabulary of groups can
canonicalise them. 28.4 % of rows keep a literal key for exactly that reason. **That the field
does not compress is itself a finding**, and it belongs next to the 5.6 % conversion-model
number: the literature does not report surface state in a form that can be joined on.

## 3. The query set

`queries/kind_e_comparability.csv`, 320 pairs, seeded (`RANDOM_SEED = 20260918`), regenerated
byte-identically by `uv run python -m mnp.eval.comparability`. A test asserts the file on disk
equals what the generator produces, and another re-derives every row's ground truth from the
table, so the answer key cannot drift from the rules.

| stratum | n | comparable | conditional | not comparable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `same_paper` | 60 | 0 | 0 | 60 |
| `cross_paper_same_material` | 60 | 0 | 0 | 60 |
| `cross_paper_different_functionalisation` | 60 | 0 | 0 | 60 |
| `cross_paper_different_material` | 30 | 0 | 0 | 30 |
| `both_conditions_complete` | 110 | 16 | 35 | 59 |
| **total** | **320** | **16** | **35** | **269** |

The first four strata are uniform seeded samples. The fifth is 60 sampled pairs **plus the
complete census** of every `comparable` and `conditionally_comparable` pair in the corpus,
unioned in rather than sampled — a stratified sample of 1.78 M pairs with 16 positives would
almost surely contain none, and an eval set of nothing but obvious noes measures nothing.

The census is exhaustive, not approximate. Every `*_unreported` rule fires unconditionally when
its field is missing in *either* row, so a row failing any of them cannot be half of a
comparable pair whatever it is paired with. That turns a pair filter into a **row** filter: 46
rows of 1 890 survive it, and C(46, 2) = 1 035 pairs need checking rather than 1 785 105. The
full 1 785 105-pair enumeration was also run once, offline, and agrees: 16 and 35.

### 4b. Leave-one-out sensitivity

Delete one rule entirely, keep the rest, recount:

| rule dropped | comparable becomes | gain |
|---|---|---|
| `quantity_mismatch` | 41 | +25 |
| `medium_text_mismatch` | 37 | +21 |
| `conversion_regime_mismatch` | 29 | +13 |
| `temperature_unreported` | 26 | +10 |
| `functionalisation_mismatch` | 24 | +8 |
| `ionic_strength_unreported` | 23 | +7 |
| `functionalisation_unreported` | 20 | +4 |
| `medium_class_mismatch` | 17 | +1 |
| *each of the other twelve* | 16 | **+0** |

`ionic_strength_unreported` blocks 98 % of pairs and deleting it buys **seven** pairs. The
rules are not a chain where one link carries the load; they are a conjunction over a corpus
that fails almost all of them at once.

Note also what the top of this table says about R1: dropping "same measurand" — pooling zeta
potentials with electrophoretic mobilities — is the single largest source of false
comparability available, +25 pairs, more than the other nineteen rules combined at the margin.
A reader who ignored the measurand would more than double the apparent answer.

### 4c. Cumulative relaxations — where the answer actually lives

| scenario | comparable | share of all pairs |
|---|---|---|
| as built | **16** | 0.0009 % |
| drop the conversion-regime family | 29 | 0.0016 % |
| drop the identity rules (polymer name is enough) | 33 | 0.0018 % |
| drop the instrument rules | 16 | 0.0009 % |
| drop all four condition-agreement tolerances (reporting only) | 51 | 0.0029 % |
| drop everything except the five reporting rules | 1 891 | 0.106 % |
| **let absence be imputed — every `*_unreported` dropped** | **81 624** | **4.57 %** |
| absence imputed *and* all agreement relaxed | 209 477 | 11.73 % |

**This is the answer to the rigging charge.** Every rule about agreement, identity, measurand
and physics, taken together, moves the count from 16 to 51. The one assumption that moves it by
five thousand times is **letting an unreported condition acquire a default**. The verdict is
not driven by strictness. It is driven by the literature's silence, and a reader who quietly
fills that silence with 25 °C, pH 7 and "water" would report 4.6 % comparability where the
truth is 0.0009 %.

That is also the precise failure mode a retrieval system is prone to: retrieval returns what is
written and has no representation for what is not.

## 5. Method — red first, then mutation

`tests/eval/test_comparability.py` was written and run before any implementation existed. First
against no module (`ImportError`, collection error), then against a null stub with the full API
and no rules: **38 failed, 14 passed**. The 14 are plumbing and symmetry assertions that a stub
satisfies; every rule assertion was red.

Two mutations were then applied to the finished implementation and reverted.

**Mutation A — an unreported condition acquires a default.** `ionic_strength_mM` falls back to
`0.0` when unreported (the "DI water means I = 0" mistake). Result: **6 failed**, including
`test_di_water_is_a_reported_medium_and_an_unreported_ionic_strength`,
`test_an_unreported_condition_never_acquires_a_default` and both query-set ground-truth tests.
Reverted; 54 pass.

**Mutation B — the reported value counts toward comparability.** `value` added to `ZetaRow`,
and identity blockers dropped when the two values are within 5 mV. Result: **7 failed**,
including all three value-guard tests and both headline pins. Reverted; 54 pass.

The first run of mutation B exposed a weak test: `test_similar_values_never_repair_a_blocked_pair`
built rows without value columns, so the mutant never fired and the test passed. It was
rewritten to build rows *with* values, the mutation re-applied, and it then failed. A test that
survives the mutation it was written for is not a test, and this one was only found because the
mutation was actually run.

## 6. What this key cannot decide

- **Whether two comparable measurements imply similar biological behaviour.** It cannot, and
  nothing derived from ζ alone can. That is Task D, not Task C.
- **Whether a particle is the same particle.** It decides whether the *reported description* is
  the same. Two papers that both write "polystyrene, carboxylated" may hold particles from
  different suppliers with different charge densities, ageing and additives. The key cannot see
  what the paper did not print, and it never pretends the absence of a difference is evidence of
  sameness.
- **Whether the conversion model was actually the same.** It checks that both rows sit in the
  same *regime*, using the instrument as a proxy for the model. A vendor default that changed
  between firmware versions is invisible here.
- **Divalent media.** `debye_length_nm` assumes a 1:1 electrolyte. A CaCl₂ or a phosphate medium
  has a shorter Debye length than the formula returns, so κa is under-estimated for those rows.
  It is used only to place a row in a regime, never to correct a value, but the assumption is
  real and it is carried into every zeta pair.
- **Particle size as such.** ζ is an intensive surface property, so the key does *not* require
  equal size; it requires only that both rows sit in the same conversion regime. One of the 16
  comparable pairs is a 120 nm against a 186 nm particle, and one is 35.2 nm against 305.5 nm
  (a mobility pair). A reader who wants "same size" must add that rule themselves — and should
  say which size, since nominal and measured are different claims.
- **Whether a paper's own comparison was sound.** 23 of the 35 `conditionally_comparable` pairs
  are within one paper, which is usually a deliberate titration. The key says the difference is
  attributable to a named condition; it does not say the paper drew the right conclusion from
  it.
- **Rows the extraction got wrong.** Every number above inherits the corpus. If a row's
  `ionic_strength_reported` is wrong, so is every verdict that row appears in.

## 7. What would change my mind

- **A cross-paper comparable pair appears** after a spelling is added to a canonicaliser. The
  count is 16 and the cross-paper count is 0; both are pinned by tests precisely so that any
  future loosening has to be argued rather than absorbed.
- **The 5 % Henry tolerance is the wrong number.** It is the least-defended constant here. If
  the right answer is 10 %, the comparable count rises — by at most 13, since that is the whole
  conversion-regime family.
- **A reviewer rejects `conditionally_comparable` as a category.** It is derived from the
  blocker set and can be deleted without touching a rule; the headline of 16 does not move.
