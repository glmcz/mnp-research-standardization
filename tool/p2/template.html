Size, charge and place

Two questions from the review call. Does a particle's zeta potential follow its size once a thousand literature values are pooled? And when microplastic surveys each count a different size window, at a different place and time, can their numbers be put side by side?

Pre-registered tests · predictions committed before the runs

Every prediction below was written down from physics, published values or other datasets, and committed () before the test ran on data nobody on the project had analysed. A verdict follows the rule committed with its prediction. Analyses chosen after seeing the results are listed separately and decide nothing.

Part 1 · zeta potential against particle size

Every dot is one zeta potential that an agent read from a paper and code matched to its line. Size is the manufacturer's nominal size where the paper gives it, otherwise the measured one. Colour is the surface chemistry the paper names.

Part 1 · polymer by polymer

Polyethylene carries no charged groups in its chain, polystyrene none either, PET and PMMA have esters. Yet in water most of their reported charges overlap. Within each plastic, size does not order the charge either. For most plastics, zeta alone cannot tell you which one you hold, or how big it is.

Part 1 · the same label, a different sign

Amine groups make a polystyrene bead positive in clean water. In the liquids the papers actually used, most reported values are negative: ions, proteins and pH change what the bead carries. The liquid decides, and the next part of the talk asks whether the liquid was reported at all.

Part 2 · where the surveys sampled

NOAA NCEI's global marine microplastics database, every record with a place. Ocean water and beach values are pieces per m³; sediment is pieces per kg dry weight. NCEI converted each study to these units itself. Soil records come from the global soil dataset (Wu et al., on Zenodo; see sources below) in items per kg, and most of them are from China, so a global soil claim leans on one country.

Part 2 · what each study could see, and what it found

Each rectangle is one study. Left to right it spans the particle sizes the method could count: the lower edge is the finest mesh or filter, the upper edge the largest size kept. Bottom to top it spans the concentrations the study reported (10th to 90th percentile in NCEI, minimum to maximum in MP-SED). The dot sits at the study's lower size limit and its median.

Part 2 · when, and how fine

Each line is one study, drawn from its first to its last sampling date at the height of its finest size limit. If the limits keep falling, a count that rises over the years can come from finer nets as much as from more plastic.
