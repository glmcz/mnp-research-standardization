## What moved

- **Row temperature precision 0.15 → 0.76.** This was the study's known-bad field: the old
  extraction recorded incubation temperatures as measurement temperatures. Rule 2 is now in
  the brief and enforced in `coerce.parse_temperature` and `zeta.verify_row`.
- **Row value precision 0.68 → 0.81**, recall 0.74 → 0.80.
- **Paper-level instrument, pH and temperature reach 1.00 / 1.00.**
- Row pH and ionic-strength precision fell (0.68 → 0.56 and 0.67 → 0.38). **Not wrong values:**
  across every matched row pair there are only four pH disagreements in the whole slice, three
  of them one case (`PMC4958399`, whether a global "all samples in PBS at pH 7.4" reaches rows
  for plasma diluted into that PBS — edge case **S14**). The precision loss comes almost
  entirely from row-count disagreements, where a worker expanded a collective statement into
  one row per particle and the annotator recorded one (**P2**, **P4**, **S17**). Those are
  granularity rulings, not extraction errors, and they are unratified.

## What the workers found that no test would have

Fourteen rule gaps (`P1`–`P7`, `S1`–`S17` in `tests/edge/key.yaml`), several raised
independently by two workers — which is the signal that a rule is missing rather than that a
worker was confused. Two became code fixes:

- A concentration belonging to another compound was attributed to a named salt
  (`"NaCl buffered with 5 mM HEPES"` → a fabricated 5 mM ionic strength). Same family as the
  `FSW + BPA (10 µM)` defect that once inflated the headline metric.
- Chinese approximation markers (`约为`, `大约`, `左右`) were unrecognised, so a hedged value
  was stored as exact.

Eleven corpus papers carry **no retrievable text at all**; `report d1` now counts them apart
from papers that were read and found to report nothing.
