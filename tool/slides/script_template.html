How the parts connect

Each act answers one question and hands the next one over. Say the bridge sentence at every boundary; it is in the cue of the last card of each act.

I · What would a standard need?

Four things travel with a number: the window, the surface, the conditions, where and when. Microplastics already took steps toward a standard; nanoplastics have not. Hands over: to count what is stated, I need a reader I do not have to trust.

II · Can an agent read the field honestly?

Only if a model reads and code decides, and only if the checks do not push the data toward absence. Hands over: the reader goes to the datasets first, the abstract’s first claim.

III · Do counts compare?

Not without the window and the place held fixed: a pooled rise that is composition, studies that differ beyond the sea’s own patchiness, samples held twice, a window that moves a count by orders of magnitude. All microplastic. Hands over: for a charge, the missing condition is the liquid.

IV · Does a charge compare?

Zeta is computed with the salt, one of five conditions papers rarely state. Pooled without them, it cannot tell plastics apart; aging shows only in a pair inside one paper; and even the one fully stated pair disagrees. Hands over: reporting is necessary, not sufficient.

V · So what should change?

Every gap next to a second laboratory, the microplastic steps taken for nanoplastics, four rules, and the equipment that removes each absence at its source.

The two streams never meet

Counts are environmental microplastic; zeta values are mostly bought beads in lab liquids. Neither is environmental nanoplastic. That is the point: nanoplastics can standardise before their data arrive, where microplastics had to repair theirs afterwards.

Four lines to know by heart

  • OpeningWhat does your agent do when the paper says nothing?

  • Design ruleLet the model read. Let code decide.
  • Last lineA number compares only with its conditions written down.

What a research audience should carry home

The room is PhD students and researchers: some measure, some model, some build tools. Nanoplastics is the case study; these four patterns travel to any field, and every act should point at one of them. Explain each the way Feynman would: the picture first, then the mechanism, then what you do not know.

Absence is a value

A field the source never states is stored as “not reported”, never filled with a plausible default. Then you can count the gap instead of hiding it, and the gap is rarely random.

Claims with addresses, decisions in code

The agent returns a value, a verbatim quote and the line it sits on. Code checks it and makes every decision after that, so nothing rests on trusting the agent.

Compare a thing with itself

A bead before and after aging, the same tow found under two studies. Pairing removes everything the two sides share, so a small effect becomes visible.

Validators create pressure

A check that flags correct values teaches agents to delete them. A flag may add doubt; it must never remove evidence.

Explain these aloud without the slides

  • Why does missing ionic strength widen a zeta value? Ionic strength sets the Debye length, the Debye length against the particle size sets Henry’s factor, and the factor turns mobility into zeta. Unknown salt, unknown factor: anywhere from {{h.f_lo}} to {{h.f_hi}}.

  • Why can’t retrieval find a comparable pair? Because comparability is a property of two rows, measured in different paragraphs or papers. No single passage contains both, so there is nothing to retrieve.

  • A paper reports one mobility at {{curve.I}} mM. Hard or soft? You cannot tell: the curves cross there by construction and split only at high salt. Draw both, and put the next measurement at the high-salt end, where hard falls toward zero and soft stops at a plateau.

  • Why does the plateau survive a corona? It is charge spread through a layer water flows through, not a potential at a shear plane, so salt cannot screen it away and a coating does not remove the plane it never needed.

  • Why is the pooled rise not a rise? Because no study sampled in both periods: the comparison mixes seas, nets and research groups. Hold the series fixed and the longest one is flat.

  • Why does the size window change a count? Particle counts follow a power law: many small, few large. A finer lower limit adds the most numerous particles, so two windows give two different counts of the same water.
