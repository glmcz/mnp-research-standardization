03Act I · why it matters

What zeta is: how fast a particle drifts in a field.

The instrument measures speed, the mobility. Zeta is computed from it with a model, and the liquid is part of that model.

I

Measure the speed.

An electric field pulls the particle through the liquid and laser light reads how fast it moves. Of the rows that name an instrument, {{zb.zetasizer}} name a Malvern Zetasizer.

II

Convert with a model.

Zeta follows from the mobility through Smoluchowski’s, Hückel’s or Henry’s formula. Which one fits depends on the particle’s size against the cloud of ions around it, and the salt in the liquid sets that cloud.

III

Print one number.

Papers print the converted zeta in {{zb.zeta_rows}} rows and the measured mobility in {{zb.mob_rows}}. What the instrument measured is mostly gone; the converted number stays, without the liquid it depends on.

out/tables/d1_zeta_table.csv: quantity, instrument

Speaker notes

For the computer scientists: zeta is not measured, it is computed. The instrument measures how fast the particle moves in a field, and a model turns that into a potential, a model that needs the salt in the liquid. Papers almost always print only the computed number.

14Act III · what the numbers say

The plan was never zeta. It was the curve.

One dot cannot tell a hard bead from a soft, coated one; a curve across salt can. The method to read the curve is built. What is missing is data: papers print dots, not curves.

I

Measure a salt series.

Mobility at a handful of salt levels, low to high. A solar panel is not rated at whatever the weather was; it is measured across temperatures and quoted at standard test conditions.

II

Fit by simulation-based inference.

A physics simulator makes many curves; a neural network learns to go back from a curve to the layer’s charge and softness, with uncertainty. Trained on {{d4.sims}} simulations, calibration passes on all {{d4.sbc_params}} parameters (lowest p {{d4.ks_min}}).

III

Report two numbers.

Mobility at one reference condition, here {{curve.I}} mM at {{curve.T}} °C, comparable across labs. And the plateau, which stays defined under a protein corona, where zeta has no shear plane.

Measured curves run through it so far: {{d4.measured}}. Full salt series exist mostly for reference particles such as sulfate latex, silica and bacteria.

src/mnp/ek/soft.py, hard.py · src/mnp/infer/figures.py same_zeta_figure · out/tables/d4_sbc.csv · CHANGELOG.md, physics demo · data/raw/digitised/

Speaker notes

The project did not start from zeta: it started from curves, because a curve across salt separates a hard bead from a soft or coated one, and one dot cannot. The physics and the neural posterior are built and calibrated on simulations, but no measured salt series has gone through them yet. That is the second half of the call: publish the curve, not the dot.

22Act V · the close

The call: a minimal reporting standard.

Report the five conditions, identify the particle, and name the conversion model. For counts, state the size window.

The five conditions

  • the medium
  • ionic strength as a number, not a buffer name
  • pH
  • the temperature of the measurement itself
  • the instrument

The particle

Supplier, lot and surface chemistry. Case 14’s two lots of {{c14.size_nominal}} nm polystyrene pass all five conditions and still differ by {{c14.gap}} mV.

The conversion model, or the curve

Smoluchowski, Hückel or Henry’s function. Without it and without κa, as in {{h.z4}} rows, a printed zeta is only known to ±{{h.hw_z4}}. Better still, report mobility itself at two or more ionic strengths: a curve, not a dot (out/checklist.md).
